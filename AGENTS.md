# AGENTS.md
Factorio 2.0 + Space Age (hard dep) colony mod. Design: `docs/factorio_colony_mod_design_document.md`. Work tracked in GitHub issues/milestones (M0 spikes+decisions gate M1-M4). Repo: alexswan10k/colony-bio-logistics. Owner uses macOS + Steam. API docs: https://lua-api.factorio.com/latest/ (always verify 2.0 signatures here).

## Layout
`mod/` mod (Lua 5.2 runtime) · `mod/scripts/model/` PURE logic · `mod/scripts/spike/` dev-only API spikes · `tests/` · `sim/` balance · `scripts/` tooling · `docs/` (SPIKES, TESTING, DEPLOYMENT, ART) · `spike-results/` run reports.

## Commands
`scripts/test.sh [filter]` unit tests (luajit) · `scripts/typecheck.sh` (LuaLS headless verification; strict missing-fields, annotated pure `model/` + `model/types.lua`, #54) · `scripts/sim.sh` balance tables · `scripts/lint-determinism.sh` · `scripts/link-dev.sh` symlink into Factorio mods · `scripts/package.sh [--release]` · `scripts/headless-spike.sh <spike[:args]>` · `scripts/save-load.sh` (#39 save/load round-trip) · `scripts/determinism.sh` (#39 two-run checksum) · `scripts/bench-colony.sh` (#39/#22 UPS gate) · `scripts/collect-results.sh` + `scripts/post-results.sh <report>` · `scripts/benchmark.sh <save>`.
Always run test.sh + lint-determinism.sh + `luajit -bl` on changed Lua before committing. Commit and push after changes.

## Rules
- Game rules go in `model/` with NO Factorio globals (`game/storage/script/defines/surface`); in-game code is a thin adapter. Test models offline; engine behaviour only via in-game spikes. Mocks encode beliefs, not evidence.
- Factorio 2.0 Lua API: Reference https://lua-api.factorio.com/latest/ for exact classes, parameters, and signatures.
- Lua 5.2: no `|`, `&`, `//`, `goto` ok. No `math.random`/`os`/`io` outside spikes (use `game.create_random_generator`). Never store functions in `storage`. Register events via `scripts.events` at load time only. Schema changes need a `store.lua` migration.
- Do not edit vanilla prototypes (opt-in design pillar). Don't scan entities per tick; use registries + `scheduler`.
- Spike/decision outcomes: record on the GitHub issue (comment + close), update docs/design doc if the design changes. Track progress via `gh issue` and project milestones.

## Decisions made
Territory = chunk-granular 32x32 (#15). Space Age required (#12). Watchtower = 1-chunk ring, must be built on claimed land (#56). Farming = player-placed plots + tile fertility table (#44 first pass). Haulers walk to every pickup (#60 follow-up): the load is claimed when the trip starts (so two haulers never count the same items) but the hauler or truck physically goes to the source - the hall/stock for supplies, the hut/crate/machine for collections - before delivering; there is no free teleport pickup. To pay for that, the hauler target is 0.35 of the town, raised to one per waiting destination (backlog of non-top-up offers) up to 0.5 (`work_plan.haul_target`), and a full 10-spoilage batch in stock is hauled at `collect` priority; compost otherwise sits above topping up already-fed houses. Taverns (#48): worker-free 2x2 building that serves ale to occupied houses within 24 tiles each evaluation (0.25 ale/resident, fractions carried in `owed`, `model/recreation.lua`); served houses are `inspired` (work trips x1/1.25) and the served share is a desirability bonus `w_recreation = 0.15`, never a penalty. Passenger rail (#49): a ride is bookkeeping, not a seat (units cannot sit in trains): colonists who would leave or are homeless walk to a Passenger Platform (serves the nearest train stop within 10 tiles) when another rail-served colony has free beds; on `on_train_changed_state` / `wait_station` riders alight first (spawned at the platform, never at their origin until 4 stops), then waiters board (entity destroyed, counted in `storage.coaches[wagon].groups`); `scripts/transit.lua` + `model/transit.lua`. Crates are plain containers (belts/inserters still work), driven by our own registry (#33). Construction = pay the ghost's `items_to_place_this` from colony stock, carry it, `revive`; repairs = one repair pack per 200 health (#37). Job locks are per-house, ore priorities are per-hut. Spoilage is cleared by colonists (free on a delivery trip, plus a haul band below every real request but above topping up already-fed houses) into a Compost Bin that batches 10 spoilage -> 1 fertilizer (#78). Salt + preserved tier (#81): Salt Pan = worker-free, shore-gated by the *same* rule as the dock (1 salt/30 s, cap 8), emptied by haulers; landlocked colonies can hand- or assembler-craft salt from stone (10 stone -> 1 salt, 10 s) so the science tree is never shore-locked; Smokehouse = the first hut with **no tool** (gated by supply, not equipment: `labour.HUTS` omits `tool`, `hut_info` reports capacity, the job returns nil without salt + raw, inputs arrive on a `request`-priority haul offer, and `keep` stops haulers carting inputs back out); 1 raw + 1 salt -> 1 preserved with 3x shelf life (45 min) and the same nutrition, because #81 is about shelf life not throughput; preserved food is eaten last. Fishing Dock placement = six water tiles in a 15-tile box, sampled once at build time (`tile.collides_with("water_tile")`, scan outward, stop early), refused-and-refunded otherwise; fishers land 5 fish per trip with a rod that wears (#80). Hunting is real engine combat against docile game: boars live on their own `colony-wildlife` force (cease-fire both ways, so no aggro and no turret fire), hunters get `defines.command.attack`, and the colonist prototype carries a ranged hitscan attack with `vision_distance` 1 so nobody picks a fight unasked; a kill is credited to the hut from `on_entity_died`'s `ev.cause` (#79). Blueprint/copy-paste persistence rides on `BlueprintEntity.tags` (one namespaced key, dense row lists, `model/tags.lua`) because `storage` is invisible to a blueprint and `LuaEntity.tags` is ghost-only (#36). Housing tiers & water economy (#43): Homestead (Tier 2, 8 colonists, 3x3, needs: food, water 0.5/occ, heat via power or coal/wood), Apartment (Tier 3, 16 colonists, 4x4, needs: food, water, mandatory electricity, comfort furniture 0.1/occ). Fast-replaceable group "colony-housing" connects tent, cabin, homestead (cabin next_upgrade -> homestead), and apartment. Water economy: Colony Well (1x1, worker-free, 1 water/10 s, cap 12, emptied by haulers) and assembler water bottling (100 water fluid -> 10 colony-water). Mid-game food & kitchens (#44): Fertilizer is hauled to Farm Huts on request-priority haul offers (up to 6 stock, kept in hut); farmers spend 1 fertilizer per fallow plot planted (`plot.fertilized = true`), doubling harvest yield (2x: 16 produce at 1.0 fertility). Colony Kitchen (1x1, 2 workers, supply-gated like Smokehouse, no tool needed) bakes 1 colony produce + 1 colony water -> 1 colony bread (60 min shelf life). Bread is eaten last in housing.FOOD. Farm hut GUI reports plot breakdown (fallow/growing/ripe/fertilized) and fertilizer stock. Colony Science (#42, #19): Custom science packs (Frontier Charter, Civic Treatise) isolated from vanilla labs via engine whitelist (can_insert/insert == 0); perishable packs spoil into defines.inventory.lab_trash and haulers clear spoilage for composting; Town Archive (3x3, cap 2 scholars) and University (4x4, cap 4 scholars) are registered labour huts with job = "research" (M.RESEARCH_SHARE = 0.15, rank 2 default); scholars staff facilities while packs are present; native custom_status diode (green staffed / red unstaffed); technologies integrate into data.raw["technology"] and progress natively. Onboarding & feedback (#85): a new game shows only the Town Hall; five scripted-trigger milestone techs (`model/milestones.lua`: Settlement = build a hall, then 4/8/12/16 colonists in the best colony) unlock the early buildings, and Food Preservation gates the pack-based tree. The Town Hall makes no tools. A Toolsmith (`model/toolsmith.lua`, staffed on the preserve job, supply-gated like the Kitchen, no tool of its own; hand-craft wood 20 + stone 10, unlocked by the first milestone) makes the tool a waiting hut needs, from the wood, iron and copper haulers bring it (3 work spells, about 30 s per tool, unlocked recipes only, and only from colony stock that holds the ingredients). It keeps one spare of each tool a built hut uses. `model/workshop.lua` holds the tool recipes. `scripts/feedback.lua` (every 300 ticks) sets `custom_status` on houses/huts/hall from `model/advice.lua` and posts native custom alerts for red problems (per-user `colony-alerts` setting); starvation kills an occupant of the starving house and names it; carried goods are drawn over colonists (`storage.carry_sprites`). Migrants still spawn at the hall: walking in from the territory edge stranded them behind water/cliffs. Motor Pool trucks (#60, #3): a car can only be driven by a character and has no autopilot, so the truck is a `unit` (`colony-truck`) that wears the vanilla car sprite and is pathed by the engine like a colonist (2.5x speed, 100-item load). A hauler whose haul is a full hand-load with more still wanted checks a car and the truck-sized load (up to 100) out of the nearest pool within 48 tiles that has a car and fuel, and claims both at checkout (a walker claims its load the same way). The driver walks to the pool and waits there under a stop order (`LuaEntity.active` is read-only in 2.0) while the truck delivers. Trips per fuel item are solid-fuel 6 / coal 2 / wood 1, and the pool burns the richest fuel it holds. The truck takes another big load while it has trips, a driver and daylight; it parks when its trips are spent, nothing big is left, or night falls. The car goes back to the pool, leftover trips stay in the pool's `tank` for the next dispatch, and a destroyed truck loses its car. Unlocked by the `colony-motor-pool` tech (30 frontier packs, needs Frontier Charter and vanilla Automobilism). Permanent death (#53): every colonist death is recorded once in `scripts/mortality.lua` - `on_entity_died` for anything that kills a colonist (cause named by `model/mortality.cause`: train, vehicle, enemy, fire...), and starvation strikes the colonist off the roster *before* `die()` and calls `mortality.record` itself, so it is never counted twice. A death joins `colony.deaths` (desirability penalty) and `colony.fallen`; the body goes to the first Cemetery with a free plot (12 each, `colony.buried`), else a walk-through `colony-grave` marker where it fell. Having a Cemetery halves the mourning half-life (6 -> 3 evaluations). A coach that *dies* kills its riders (deaths in their origin colony); one removed any other way (`on_object_destroyed` without a death) returns them to their hall. Civil defence (#61): the trigger is `on_entity_damaged` by the enemy force on anything of a colony's force inside its territory (no peacetime scan), which raises or refreshes an alarm (`model/alarm.lua`, `scripts/shelter.lua`); colonists within 40 tiles run to the nearest house/hall at least 24 tiles from the threat and are `destructible = false` while sheltered ("indoors"); the alarm is held by enemy units within 40 tiles for at most 60 s past the last hit (a quiet nest cannot pin the town indoors) and clears 10 s after they leave; a colonist comes out when no alarm covers the spot it fled from. Watchtower garrison (#56 first slice): the tower is also a labour hut (`job = "guard"`, tool `colony-rifle`, capacity 4), and the rifles in its stock room are the armory - one post per rifle, hauled out of the hall at `tools` priority, with the hut limit (default **0**) deciding how many of those posts the player mans; an unmanned tower asks for nothing and never nags, and the Toolsmith only makes a rifle for a *manned* tower. A guard walks to the tower's door and holds it under a stop order with an always-visible label, scanning `garrison.RANGE` (16 tiles) every ~1 s (`garrison.CHECK_TICKS`, jittered per colonist) for the nearest enemy *unit* (biters/spitters, never worms or nests) and attacking it; engagement needs no alarm, and guards are exempt from sheltering (`alarm.EXEMPT`) so a manned tower keeps shooting while the town hides. One completed engagement wears a rifle one step exactly like a hunter's trip (`labour.TOOL_USES`); a worn-out rifle is consumed from the tower, its post closes and that guard walks off, so a garrison cannot outlive its weapons. `guard` is in `jobs.JOBS`/`DEFAULT` (rank 2), so the priority matrix, headcount targets, per-hut locks and the open-jobs pull all apply; guards sleep at night and are re-posted in the morning, and lowering the limit disbands the garrison while the rifles stay put (`model/garrison.lua`, `scripts/work.lua`). A hauler request is never served from the requester's own stock room (`haul_plan.match`), or a tower would carry its own rifles out and straight back in. Commuter outposts (#50, rules decided on #21): an outpost is a colony with a Canteen (its own hall for stock/tools, no houses needed); it wants ceil(hut capacity / 0.65) commuters less those there, only by day, with food in the canteen or hall larder and a served platform. Idle colonists of other towns on the surface commute; coach groups carry `kind` ("commute" out, "return" home with `home`); commuters at work are the outpost's colonists (`storage.commuters[un] = {home, ends}`) while the home holds their beds in `colony.away`; they eat at the Canteen (then the hall larder), and go home at nightfall, after 12000 ticks or when unfed; stranded 7200 ticks they walk home. `scripts/commute.lua` + `model/commute.lua`. Supply & demand (#90): demand is a graph, not a flat count. `model/supply_graph.lua` walks the force's *unlocked* recipes upward from the seeds the colony already had (opted-in machine requests, demand-crate filters, hut `inputs`) - one recipe per product, the cheapest path (fewest ingredient types, ties by name) - for `DEPTH = 4` floors at `DAMPING = 0.75` per floor, so a pack request raises plates, gears, cable and ore without the player setting a quota for every intermediate. `quota_target = base + demand` is unchanged, so a player-set material cap (#84) stays a hard ceiling: `work.supply_capped` compares stock against the *base* target, while demand only raises the collection target for uncapped items. The graph is rebuilt lazily per force (`GRAPH_TTL` 1800 ticks), the demand table per colony (`DEMAND_TTL` 30 ticks, dropped by `set_quota`/`set_machine`), so the hot path is a table lookup, not a graph walk. It is a feedstock heuristic, not a production planner: it answers "how much of this should the town want", never "what should be built", and a recipe that consumes its own output is bounded by depth and damping. Distance-aware picking (#93): a hut is chosen least-staffed first, then nearest the colonist's house; a harvest site inside the hut uses an 8-tile distance band and the colonist's house only breaks ties inside that band (`model/sites.lua` - the walk hut -> site -> hut dominates); a haul source is chosen by the route the hauler actually walks (`dist(hauler, src) + dist(src, dst)`), not by proximity to the destination; and the mean house-to-work walk feeds `w_commute = 0.05` into desirability (saturating at 48 tiles, workers only, so no job is never a penalty). A hut's food already goes straight to a house that wants it (a direct transfer beats storage within the same band); `m1_haul_dist` pins that, and it also shows the one case that still rounds-trips: at start-up a hut can be emptied into the hall before colonists have moved into the houses.

Material priorities (#91): colony-wide rank for what the town cares about right now. `colony.ratings[item] = {rank = 1..5, enabled = bool}` (schema 34), 1 = most wanted, 3 = neutral/default, 5 = least; rank expresses order not stop (use quotas/caps to stop). Scope is harvested goods + produced intermediates (quota items + supply items, not tools), default all 3 so untouched towns are unchanged and #90 demand breaks ties. `work.colony_missing_list` returns missing harvest jobs ordered by best material rank (via `material_priority.best`) then demand, consumed by `work_plan.choose` (map form keeps legacy round-robin; ranked list is deterministic best-first and still honours the unstaffed-first-worker rule). Per-hut ore priority stays the local override; `work.eff_ore_groups` falls back to `material_priority.mine_rank` (1..5, disabled = 6) when a hut has no explicit rank, so iron goes first without clicking every hut. `haul_plan.match`/`order` take an optional `rank_ctx = {ratings, demand}` and tie-break same-band offers by material rank after building priority (deterministic: rank, deficit, distance, key) so a town that cares about iron hauls iron first. Town Hall panel has a Material priorities table (enable checkbox + 1..5 controls per rankable item, demand column, reset) round-tripping `colony.ratings`; `m1_material` proves a miner picks iron over closer copper at equal quotas. Metal tool tiers (#95): woodcutter/miner/farm have a steel ladder (`colony-steel-axe/pickaxe/hoe`, 2 iron + 1 steel, 0 wood, 45 uses at 0.8x work ticks vs tier-1 30/1.0x), unlocked by the new `colony-steel-tools` tech (30 frontier packs, Logistics); hut wear follows the best tool present and consumes tier-1 first so upgrades stick, the Toolsmith covers tier-1 gaps before steel spares and only buffers steel while steel is wanted, and haul delivers the best tier in stock — wood per colonist-hour falls to 0 for upgraded huts, shifting demand to iron/steel (#90). Basket/rod stay single-tier.

Illness (#47, decided on #20): per-colonist `storage.sick[un] = {kind, age}`, rolled once per colony evaluation with a deterministic hash (`model/illness.roll`), no contagion; Smoker's Lung from the colony's pollution (fatal after 40 untreated evaluations), Food Poisoning when a house eats food >= 75% spoiled (passes after 4); the sick work at half speed and cost desirability (`w_sickness` 0.3); a Clinic is a labour hut with the `heal` job (doctors on duty while it holds Herbal Medicine and someone is sick), each doctor cures 2 per evaluation at 1 medicine; `scripts/illness.lua` + `model/illness.lua`. Harvest priority: resource quotas default to 200 (0 = unlimited; a hut whose quotas are met has no slots). Produced intermediates (plates, gears, brick, concrete, circuits, plastic, solid fuel) also carry a ledger quota, default **0 = unlimited**; a player-set cap is a *hard* ceiling on collection (`work.supply_capped` compares stock against the **base** target, so #90 demand can never raise a number the player typed): at the cap haulers stop emptying machine output, the output backs up and `work.refresh_machine` marks the machine `capped` so it stops asking for inputs. Without a cap (0 = unlimited) `quota_target = base + resource_demand`. `work.colony_missing_jobs` marks empty stock (strictly first, but food below target always joins) and below-quota stock as missing; `work_plan.choose` staffs every role once, then unstaffed missing roles first, then round-robins missing roles (`colony.harvest_cursor`), then the usual shares. A harvester leaves its post for a missing role only if its hut keeps another worker, and only towns under `work.SMALL_TOWN` check waiting deliveries on the keep-post fast path (#22). The first settler arrives at an empty hall even without food; later migrants need food. Tents, desirability, migration & breeding (#88): a Tent holds **1** colonist; every tent drags desirability down (`w_tents = 0.05`, stops growing at `tent_cap` = 8) so tent towns plateau and Cabins are the next step. Migration fills to `BOOTSTRAP_SETTLERS` = 4 colonists, then five times slower; the empty hall still gets its first settler. Houses are most desirable empty or single-occupancy (`w_privacy = 0.15`), open job slots are a big pull factor (`w_jobs = 0.35`, full at `jobs_full` = 6 slots). Breeding is the long-term growth path: one adult of each sex in one house, provisions met, a free bed, no birth in the last `COOLDOWN_TICKS`; babies count toward occupancy, eat half portions, never work, and grow after `CHILD_TICKS` (a day). Sex is parity of unit number, no storage; children tracked in `storage.age` (schema 28). Sleeping (#86): units cannot enter buildings, so colonists sleep at a standable point right at the house (`door_point`, `find_non_colliding_position` next to the building, falling back to the centre) with an always-visible 💤 label; going to bed returns any carried load to the hall instead of dropping it. Harvesting (#87): colonists stand right at the edge of the thing they are working (`site_radius`, from the prototype's selection box) with a persistent ring for the whole trip. Production statistics (#89): the engine only counts engine-driven transfers, so Lua insert/remove is invisible to the production GUI; the mod records its own flows via `LuaFlowStatistics::on_flow` on the hall's force and surface — positive = input (production), negative = output (consumption), plain prototype names as ids, merged at end of tick (`scripts/stats.lua`); headless proof in `m1_stats`. Playthrough ergonomics & firearms: all colony buildings (huts, houses, crates, machines) carry logistics enabled checkboxes and building priorities (1-5, colony 3 default, external machines 4 default); storage buildings (Town Hall, Warehouse) carry configurable storage priority (default 3), and Warehouses support accept-all toggling and item whitelists; workers stay in position via `command.stop`; blueprint construction is snappy (`construct.WORK_TICKS = 30`) with collision-safe destination entity pathing; early hunting and watchtower defense use bows and arrows (`colony-bow` 4 wood, `colony-arrow` 10 for 1 wood) and the colony rifle (unlocked with Colony Logistics), while vanilla firearms need no colony research: haul a pistol or submachine gun plus magazines to a lodge or tower and it is used (`garrison.PREFERENCE` rifle > submachine gun > pistol > bow; a gun spends a magazine every ten engagements, a bow an arrow per kill, a rifle wears like a tool); spoilage carried at nightfall diverts directly to the nearest compost bin; colonists sleep inside their houses; ghost configs are cleanly instanced via tags; resource quotas stop harvesting immediately to redirect labor to hauling; research-driven tech tree with Frontier Charter packs and non-water/Gleba recipes replaces pop-gated milestones. Open decisions: #21 tourism (commuting is decided). #18 (housing) and #22 (perf budget) are decided and closed; the onboarding pass is tracked in #85 with an in-game checklist that feeds the #54 playtest.
